How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Dunwoody?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Dunwoody?

Typical Cost at a Glance

In Dunwoody, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. Rebuilding after a sewage backup or a serious flood can push the cost far past $10,000.

None of this is a quote, only the usual range across the country. Local labor rates and material prices in Dunwoody, GA move the numbers up or down. An exact price requires someone to inspect the home in person and take moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. There are three categories in the industry standard, and each reflects a level of contamination. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterSewage, river flooding, storm surgeRoughly $7 to $7.50

Category can change. Clean water that sits for a couple of days picks up bacteria and is then treated as gray or black water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

What Drives the Price

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. A leak found in an hour costs far less than one found after a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Afterward comes a second cost for new flooring, drywall, paint and cabinets, which on larger jobs often exceeds the first.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

A daily rate is common for dehumidifiers and air movers. If your house is dry after three days, you pay less than if it needs six.

Price Ranges by Type of Job

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in Dunwoody, national figures usually look like this. They are a place to begin, and an actual price will differ.

  • Burst pipe. Often $1,500 to $5,000 for cleanup and drying, plus the burst pipe repair itself. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. A common range is $3,000 to $10,000 or higher. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Dunwoody?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. Months of dripping from a fitting may be labeled a maintenance issue by the insurer. Outside flooding, for instance from storm surge or a river that rises, is usually not covered without separate flood insurance. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Check your policy or call your agent before you assume anything. Your deductible also matters. A small repair may come in under the deductible, in which case filing may not be worthwhile.

How to Keep the Bill Down

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. A job with active growth takes more days and costs more money.
  • Move what you can. Furniture, rugs and boxes should come up off wet flooring, provided that is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. The risk of being shocked or getting sick outweighs anything you might save. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

How to Get an Exact Price

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.

If you need water damage restoration in Dunwoody, GA, the best time to ask for that inspection is now, while the damage is still small.
